# Hyperkalaemia — Summary

_Demo reference summary. Verify against local protocols._

## Definitions
Hyperkalaemia is a serum potassium above the laboratory upper limit (commonly > 5.0–5.5 mmol/L). Mild: 5.5–5.9; moderate: 6.0–6.4; severe: ≥ 6.5 mmol/L or any level with ECG changes.
Pseudohyperkalaemia from haemolysis, prolonged tourniquet use or high platelet counts should be excluded with a repeat sample when the result is unexpected.

## Common causes
Reduced kidney function, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, potassium-sparing diuretics, NSAIDs, trimethoprim, heparin, potassium supplements and salt substitutes, and metabolic acidosis.

## Management principles
Severe hyperkalaemia or ECG changes is an emergency: cardiac membrane stabilisation with intravenous calcium, shift therapy (insulin with glucose, nebulised salbutamol), and potassium removal.
For chronic mild hyperkalaemia in CKD, review contributing medications and diet, correct metabolic acidosis, consider loop or thiazide diuretics where appropriate, and consider potassium binders (patiromer, sodium zirconium cyclosilicate) to allow continuation of renin–angiotensin system inhibitors that offer kidney and cardiac protection.
