# Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) — Summary

_Demo reference summary aligned with KDIGO concepts. Verify against current KDIGO guidance._

## Definition and staging
CKD is abnormal kidney structure or function present for more than 3 months. Staging uses GFR category (G1 ≥ 90; G2 60–89; G3a 45–59; G3b 30–44; G4 15–29; G5 < 15 mL/min/1.73m²) and albuminuria category (A1 UACR < 30 mg/g; A2 30–300 mg/g; A3 > 300 mg/g).
A sustained eGFR decline of more than 5 mL/min/1.73m² per year, or a confirmed change to a worse GFR category with a ≥ 25% drop, indicates progression.

## Kidney-protective therapy
- Blood pressure: a systolic target below 120 mmHg is suggested when tolerated, measured with standardised technique.
- ACE inhibitor or ARB at maximally tolerated dose for patients with albuminuria (A2–A3), especially with diabetes or hypertension. Check creatinine and potassium within 2–4 weeks of starting or increasing the dose; a rise in creatinine of up to 30% is commonly accepted.
- SGLT2 inhibitors are recommended for patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD with eGFR ≥ 20 mL/min/1.73m², and for many patients with CKD without diabetes.
- A non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (finerenone) may be added in type 2 diabetes with albuminuria and normal potassium.
- Statin therapy is recommended for adults aged 50 or older with CKD.

## Medication safety
Review drug doses as GFR falls: metformin (reduce at eGFR 30–44, stop below 30), NSAIDs (avoid), gadolinium and iodinated contrast (risk assessment), and renally cleared antibiotics and anticoagulants.

## Referral to nephrology
Consider referral for eGFR below 30, persistent UACR above 300 mg/g, rapid progression, refractory hypertension, persistent hyperkalaemia, or unexplained haematuria.
