# Primary Hypothyroidism — Summary

_Demo reference summary. Verify against current endocrine society guidance._

## Diagnosis
Primary hypothyroidism is characterised by an elevated TSH with a low free T4. Subclinical hypothyroidism is an elevated TSH with a normal free T4; repeat testing in 6–12 weeks is advised before treatment decisions because transient elevations are common.

## Treatment
Levothyroxine is the treatment of choice. A full replacement dose is approximately 1.6 micrograms per kg per day in otherwise healthy adults; older adults and people with coronary disease are usually started on a lower dose (for example 25–50 micrograms daily) and titrated.
Levothyroxine should be taken on an empty stomach, 30–60 minutes before breakfast, separated from calcium, iron supplements and proton-pump inhibitors by at least 4 hours because these reduce absorption.

## Monitoring
Check TSH 6–8 weeks after starting therapy or changing dose. Once stable, TSH is typically checked every 6–12 months. The usual goal is a TSH within the laboratory reference range.

## Special situations
Iron deficiency and oral iron therapy can both affect levothyroxine absorption. Pregnancy increases requirements, often by 25–30%, and requires early dose adjustment and closer TSH monitoring.
