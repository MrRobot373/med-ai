# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus — Management Summary

_Demo reference summary. Verify against current national/ADA/IDF guidance._

## Diagnosis
Diabetes is diagnosed with any of: HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L), 2-hour plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL during an oral glucose tolerance test, or random glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms. In the absence of unequivocal hyperglycaemia, an abnormal result should be confirmed by repeat testing.

## Glycaemic targets
An HbA1c target below 7% is appropriate for many non-pregnant adults. Less stringent targets (for example below 8%) may suit people with limited life expectancy, frequent or severe hypoglycaemia, advanced complications or extensive comorbidity. Targets should be individualised and reviewed regularly. HbA1c is typically checked every 3 months when not at target and at least twice yearly when stable.

## Pharmacological therapy
Metformin remains a common first-line agent when tolerated and when eGFR allows; metformin should not be started when eGFR is below 45 mL/min/1.73m² and is contraindicated below 30 mL/min/1.73m².
In people with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease, an SGLT2 inhibitor and/or a GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven benefit is recommended independent of HbA1c.
SGLT2 inhibitors are preferred for patients with CKD (eGFR ≥ 20) or heart failure. GLP-1 receptor agonists are preferred when weight reduction or atherosclerotic risk reduction is a priority.
Insulin is indicated with severe hyperglycaemia (HbA1c > 10% or glucose ≥ 300 mg/dL), catabolic features, or when other agents fail to achieve targets.

## Monitoring for complications
- Kidney: annual urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and eGFR; more often when CKD is present.
- Eyes: dilated retinal examination at diagnosis and then every 1–2 years if no retinopathy.
- Feet: annual comprehensive foot examination including monofilament testing.
- Cardiovascular: blood pressure at every visit; lipid profile at diagnosis and periodically. Most adults with diabetes aged 40–75 should receive statin therapy.

## Hypoglycaemia
Level 1 hypoglycaemia is glucose below 70 mg/dL; level 2 is below 54 mg/dL. Sulfonylureas and insulin carry the highest risk; review doses when eGFR declines.
