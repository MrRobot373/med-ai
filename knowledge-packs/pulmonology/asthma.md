# Asthma in Adults — Summary

_Demo reference summary aligned with GINA concepts. Verify against current GINA guidance._

## Diagnosis
Variable respiratory symptoms (wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness, cough) with documented variable expiratory airflow limitation — for example a bronchodilator increase in FEV1 of > 12% and > 200 mL from baseline.

## Treatment approach
Short-acting beta-agonist (SABA)-only treatment is no longer recommended for adults and adolescents because of the risk of severe exacerbations.
Preferred track: as-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol for mild asthma; maintenance-and-reliever therapy (MART) with ICS-formoterol for moderate asthma, stepping up to medium-dose maintenance.
Before stepping up, check inhaler technique, adherence, exposures (smoking, allergens, occupational triggers) and comorbidities such as rhinitis, obesity and gastro-oesophageal reflux.

## Severe asthma
Uncontrolled asthma despite high-dose ICS-LABA, or requiring this to stay controlled. Assess phenotype (blood eosinophils, FeNO, IgE); biologic therapy targeting IgE, IL-5/IL-5R, IL-4R or TSLP may be considered after specialist assessment.

## Exacerbation red flags
Talking in words only, SpO₂ < 90%, PEF < 50% of best or predicted, silent chest, confusion or drowsiness require emergency care.
