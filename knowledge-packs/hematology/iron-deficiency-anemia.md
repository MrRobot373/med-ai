# Iron Deficiency Anaemia — Summary

_Demo reference summary. Verify against current haematology/gastroenterology guidance._

## Diagnosis
Anaemia is haemoglobin below the laboratory reference range (commonly < 13 g/dL in men and < 12 g/dL in non-pregnant women). Iron deficiency is supported by a low serum ferritin; a ferritin below 30 ng/mL is a commonly used threshold, but ferritin is an acute-phase reactant and can be falsely normal during inflammation, where transferrin saturation below 20% helps.
Microcytosis (low MCV) is typical but not universal.

## Investigation of cause
Men and post-menopausal women with iron deficiency anaemia should be assessed for gastrointestinal blood loss, including bidirectional endoscopy when appropriate. In pre-menopausal women, assess menstrual losses and diet; endoscopy is considered with gastrointestinal symptoms, family history of colorectal cancer or persistent deficiency despite replacement. Coeliac serology is recommended.

## Treatment
Oral iron (for example ferrous sulfate) once daily or on alternate days improves absorption and tolerability. Separate from levothyroxine, calcium and antacids. A haemoglobin rise of about 1–2 g/dL within 4 weeks indicates response. Continue for about 3 months after haemoglobin normalises to replenish stores.
Intravenous iron is considered when oral iron is not tolerated or ineffective, with malabsorption, CKD, heart failure or when rapid repletion is needed.
