# Atrial Fibrillation — Anticoagulation and Rate Control Summary

_Demo reference summary. Verify against current ACC/AHA/ESC guidance._

## Stroke risk
Stroke risk is commonly estimated with CHA₂DS₂-VASc (congestive heart failure 1, hypertension 1, age ≥ 75 years 2, diabetes 1, prior stroke/TIA 2, vascular disease 1, age 65–74 years 1, female sex 1). Oral anticoagulation is recommended for a score of ≥ 2 in men or ≥ 3 in women and should be considered at 1 (men) or 2 (women).

## Anticoagulant choice
Direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban, dabigatran) are preferred over warfarin for most patients, except those with moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis or a mechanical heart valve.
Apixaban standard dose is 5 mg twice daily; the dose is reduced to 2.5 mg twice daily when at least two of the following are present: age ≥ 80 years, body weight ≤ 60 kg, serum creatinine ≥ 1.5 mg/dL.
Bleeding risk should be assessed and modifiable risk factors corrected (uncontrolled hypertension, concomitant antiplatelets or NSAIDs, excess alcohol); a high bleeding score alone is not a reason to withhold anticoagulation.

## Rate control
Beta-blockers are first-line for rate control, particularly in HFrEF. Non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers should be avoided when LVEF ≤ 40%. A resting heart rate below 110 bpm is an acceptable initial target in many patients.
