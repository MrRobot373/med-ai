# Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF) — Summary

_Demo reference summary. Verify against current ACC/AHA/HFSA or ESC guidance._

## Definition
HFrEF: symptoms and signs of heart failure with LVEF ≤ 40%. HF with improved EF: previous LVEF ≤ 40% with a follow-up measurement > 40%.

## Guideline-directed medical therapy (four pillars)
1. ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan) preferred; ACE inhibitor or ARB if ARNI is not possible.
2. Evidence-based beta-blocker: bisoprolol, carvedilol or sustained-release metoprolol succinate.
3. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (spironolactone or eplerenone) if eGFR > 30 and potassium < 5.0 mmol/L.
4. SGLT2 inhibitor (dapagliflozin or empagliflozin).
All four classes should be started and titrated promptly as tolerated. Loop diuretics are used for congestion at the lowest effective dose.

## Monitoring
Check potassium and kidney function 1–2 weeks after starting or up-titrating RAAS inhibitors or MRAs. Natriuretic peptides (BNP, NT-proBNP) support diagnosis and prognosis; a falling level with therapy is generally favourable. Reassess LVEF 3–6 months after optimisation to guide device decisions (ICD, CRT).

## Drugs to avoid
NSAIDs, non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers (diltiazem, verapamil), thiazolidinediones and most class I antiarrhythmics worsen outcomes in HFrEF.
